Collgar Wind Farm, WA · lightning data review · prepared 9 Nov 2026

Is Collgar's lightning data good enough to tell which turbine was struck?

A statistical comparison of the AccuWeather and Vaisala (xWeather) stroke data at Collgar, and a test of whether negative cloud-to-ground strokes visibly attach to turbines, benchmarked against Rush Springs, a comparable US wind farm.

Short answer: no. At Rush Springs (Oklahoma) the same analysis finds 19 times more −CG strokes within 50 m of a turbine than chance, because strokes there are located to about 30 m. At Collgar the stroke pattern is indistinguishable from a farm with no turbines. That is what we would expect if Collgar's location error is 0.5–1.4 km. Showing turbine attachment needs an error below about 100–150 m, and pinning a strike to one turbine needs below about 100 m. The AccuWeather feed does not help: for {{same_frac}} of its records it is the same Vaisala solution, re-delivered with a 12-hour clock error and rounded values.

1. AccuWeather vs Vaisala at Collgar

Data. AccuWeather: collgar_accuweather_20251103_20260809.csv, 6,525 records within 50 km (time, lat/lon, kA, count). Vaisala: the xWeather realtime day-files in the WGLT archive for the same period (17 storm days, CG and IC, with error ellipses, sensor counts and χ²). Comparison domain: within 50 km of the farm centre, on the 17 days both sources hold. That gives 6,109 AccuWeather records against 26,722 Vaisala strokes (7,548 CG, 19,174 IC). AccuWeather also has 96 records on 13 days for which the archive holds no Vaisala file. Those are excluded.

Matching. AccuWeather times are truncated to whole seconds. After the +12 h correction, matched pairs fall at Δt = −1 to 0 s ({{trunc_frac}} of pairs). Each AccuWeather record was paired one-to-one with the nearest Vaisala stroke in that one-second window, exact positional twins first, up to 10 km away.

The three days where the feeds disagree

On 4–5 Nov 2025 and 10 Aug 2026 the clock still lines up at +12 h, but none of AccuWeather's 1,190 records is a positional copy of a Vaisala stroke. AccuWeather was evidently fed a different location solution of the same strokes on those days. They are the only independent location check available at Collgar.

  • Separation from the nearest Vaisala CG in the same second (within 5 km): median {{iv_med}} m, interquartile {{iv_q1}}–{{iv_q3}} m. Only {{iv_500}} are within 500 m and {{iv_1k}} within 1 km.
  • Peak current agrees poorly (r = {{iv_r}}, median |ΔkA| {{iv_dka}} kA). Polarity agrees {{iv_pol}} of the time.
  • If the two solutions were independent, a median separation of {{iv_med}} m implies a combined 1-σ error of about {{iv_sig}} m. Two things cloud that figure. Pairing errors in busy seconds push it up. Errors shared because both solutions use the same sensors push it down. Read it as a rough lower bracket on Collgar's real error. Vaisala's own 50% ellipse for these strokes has a median semi-major of {{iv_sm}} m, which corresponds to σ ≈ 1.2–1.4 km.

What this means for the two sources

  • Location. AccuWeather adds no independent location information. Most of the time it is the Vaisala realtime position. On the days it differs, its positions are no closer to the turbines and carry no error estimate.
  • Stroke parameters. Peak current is Vaisala's value, truncated to whole kA. There is no rise time, multiplicity, charge, specific energy or continuing-current estimate. Vaisala carries all of these, though rise time is missing for about 55% of Collgar strokes.
  • Completeness. AccuWeather holds about two-thirds of Vaisala's CG strokes and almost none of its IC pulses. Counts built from it understate the stroke rate.
  • Timing. Any use of the AccuWeather file must add 12 hours to “Date & Time GMT”. Its one-second resolution cannot separate the strokes of a multi-stroke flash.
  • Note for the draft report: Collgar Blade Lightning Damage and Site Lightning Assessment, DRAFT Rev A calls this file the “Weatherzone feed” (6,525 records, 3 Nov 2025 – 9 Aug 2026). It is the AccuWeather file. The draft's 12-hour finding is confirmed here. Its 73% containment figure used a 25 km radius and the realtime day list; this analysis uses 50 km and separates same-solution matches from different-solution ones.

2. Do −CG strokes attach visibly to turbines? Collgar vs Rush Springs

Why Rush Springs. It is the closest high-data US analogue in the WGLT fleet: 119 turbines of a 2 MW class (GE 2.0-116, 138 m tip, against Collgar's V90-2.0 at 125 m), ~400 m turbine spacing, and 73 m of ground relief against Collgar's 50 m. It sits inside the dense US NLDN network, and its location error has been measured from turbine strikes at σ = 28 m.

Is the data accurate enough? Three tests

  1. Collgar should have shown a signal if the data were good. Scaling Rush Springs' attachment rate (per unit background stroke density, by tip height²) gives Collgar an equivalent capture radius of about {{rc}} m. That means roughly {{natt}} −CG strokes captured by turbines over the record, {{natt_pct}} of strokes within 5 km. The ridge would push this higher.
  2. Simulated attachment at Collgar. Strokes in the real Collgar data that fall within the capture radius were moved onto the turbine, every stroke was blurred by a chosen location error, and the result was tested against the shifted-layout null. The signal is found every time at σ ≤ 100 m, 57% of the time at 250 m, 26% at 500 m, and 8% at 1,445 m. The last figure is the false-alarm rate, meaning no detection at all.
  3. Rush Springs degraded to Collgar quality. Rush Springs strokes were given extra location error and subsampled to Collgar's stroke count. Its unmistakable signal survives 250 m of added error (93% detected), drops to 33% at 500 m and 12% at 1 km, and vanishes at 1,445 m (3%).

So the null result at Collgar does not mean turbines there are not struck. It means a 0.5–1.4 km error erases the evidence. Pinning an individual strike to a turbine is harder still. With Collgar's layout, the nearest turbine to a reported stroke is the struck one {{att100}} of the time at σ = 100 m, {{att300}} at 300 m, {{att500}} at 500 m, and {{att1445}} at 1,445 m.

Conclusions and recommendations

  1. Neither source can attribute strikes to Collgar turbines. Vaisala realtime data at Collgar has an estimated 1-σ error between ~460 m (independent re-location, lower bracket) and ~1,445 m (Vaisala's ellipses). Detecting attachment needs about 100–150 m, and per-turbine attribution about 100 m: a gap of 4–15×.
  2. AccuWeather is not an independent second opinion. It is a truncated, CG-only subset of the same Vaisala realtime solution ({{same_frac}} exact copies), with a 12-hour timestamp error. It should not be used to corroborate Vaisala locations, and its peak currents carry no extra information.
  3. Use Vaisala, with its ellipses, for site-level statistics. That covers flash density, seasonality, polarity, peak-current distribution and storm windows. Treat every turbine inside a stroke's 95% ellipse as a candidate, as the draft report already does.
  4. For attribution, measure at the turbine. Turbine-mounted strike detection (current sensors, strike counters or acoustic blade sensors) is the only way to tie damage to a strike at Collgar. Check with Vaisala whether a reprocessed or regional-sensor solution with better geometry exists for south-west WA. At Rush Springs, reprocessing is part of what delivers 28 m.

Caveats

  • Rush Springs data are 99.96% reprocessed and Collgar's are 100% realtime. Part of the accuracy gap is the solution type, not just network geometry. That only strengthens the conclusion for Collgar's realtime data.
  • The attachment-rate transfer assumes US downward-lightning physics at Collgar. A 2× higher or lower rate shifts the detectable σ only modestly: power at σ ≥ 1 km stays near the false-alarm rate.
  • The WGLT turbine table has 127 Collgar positions. The draft report counts 111 operating turbines. Using 111 would slightly reduce the expected signal and change nothing else.
  • The archive holds Vaisala files only for storm days the pipeline pulled. 96 AccuWeather records on 13 other days could not be checked.
  • Rush Springs and Collgar strokes are from each farm's WGLT database (CG strokes ≤ ~25–30 km). The location analysis uses negative CG only.
